# Seasonal Agriculture Performance Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes agricultural performance across different seasons, crops, states, environmental conditions, resource usage, and economic factors.

The analysis aims to identify seasonal patterns, relationships, variations, and meaningful insights that can support data-driven agricultural planning.

## Objectives

- Analyze agricultural performance across different seasons.
- Clean and prepare the agricultural dataset.
- Compare crop yield and profit across seasons.
- Analyze relationships between rainfall, fertilizer usage, water efficiency, and crop yield.
- Study regional and crop-wise variations.
- Identify meaningful patterns and insights from the dataset.
- Provide data-driven recommendations for agricultural planning.

## Dataset

The dataset contains information related to:

- State and District
- Crop and Season
- Farm Area
- Rainfall and Temperature
- Humidity and Sunlight
- Soil Conditions
- Fertilizer and Pesticide Usage
- Irrigation Methods
- Crop Yield and Production
- Market Price
- Cost, Revenue and Profit
- Water Usage and Water Efficiency
- Disease and Pest Risk

## Key Insights

- Kharif season recorded the highest average yield of **5.64 tonnes/ha**.
- Kharif season recorded the highest average profit of approximately **INR 179,367**.
- Rainfall showed a very weak correlation with crop yield (**r = 0.031**).
- Fertilizer usage showed almost no linear correlation with crop yield (**r = 0.001**).
- Water efficiency showed a moderate positive correlation with profit (**r = 0.490**).
- Sugarcane recorded the highest average yield at **46.94 tonnes/ha**.
- Maharashtra recorded the highest average profit at approximately **INR 136,874**.
- Rainfed irrigation recorded the highest average water efficiency at **7.57 tonnes per 1000 m³**.

## Recommendations

- Consider seasonal variations while planning agricultural activities.
- Improve water-use efficiency wherever suitable.
- Monitor rainfall and soil moisture conditions for irrigation planning.
- Consider crop-wise yield differences during crop selection.
- Optimize fertilizer usage according to crop and seasonal requirements.
- Consider regional differences in agricultural profitability.
- Use data-driven insights for better agricultural decision-making.

## Limitations

The analysis is based on the variables and observations available in the given dataset. Correlation indicates relationships between variables but does not establish causation. Seasonal patterns may also vary under different environmental, economic, and market conditions.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how data analytics and visualization techniques can be used to understand seasonal agricultural performance, identify important relationships, compare different farming conditions, and generate evidence-based insights for agricultural planning.
